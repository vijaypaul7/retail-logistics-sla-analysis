Retail Logistics SLA & Delivery Performance Analysis

Overview

This project analyses shipment performance using Excel-based operational analytics.

The objective was to identify patterns in delivery performance, evaluate SLA compliance, and determine which shipment segments contribute most significantly to observed delays.

Business Questions

The analysis focused on:

What proportion of shipments meet the delivery SLA?
How is shipment volume distributed across weight classes?
Which weight categories experience the highest delay rates?
Which shipment segments contribute most to overall delays?
Does shipment mode provide additional context around delivery performance?

Key Findings

Overall SLA Performance

* Total shipments analysed: 10,998
* On-time shipments: 59.67%
* Delayed shipments: 40.33%

Weight Distribution

* Heavy: 54.25%
* Light: 29.51%
* Medium: 16.25%

Delivery Performance by Weight

Weight Class	Late %	On-Time %
Heavy         56.8%   	43.2%
Light	        32.2%.   	67.8%
Medium      	0.1%	    99.9%

Heavy shipments were the largest contributor to observed delays, accounting for 76.4% of all late shipments.

Shipment Mode

Shipment mode was analysed alongside weight class to identify concentration patterns within delayed shipments.
Heavy shipments transported by Ship represented 52.0% of all observed late shipments in the analysed dataset.

Analytical Approach

The project used:

* Excel Tables
* Data cleaning
* Duplicate-record handling
* Conditional formulas
* PivotTables
* Filtering and segmentation
* KPI calculation
* Percentage analysis
* Operational interpretation

Business Interpretation

The analysis identifies Heavy shipments as the primary segment requiring further operational investigation.

The results indicate a strong association between Heavy shipment classification and observed delays. However, the analysis does not establish causation. Further investigation would be required across shipment mode, carrier, route, customer service interactions and other operational variables.
